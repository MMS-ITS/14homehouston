Rental Property Due Diligence: Master Template
Version 1.0, 4 October 2026. MMS Consortium. One method for every long-term rental purchase.

1. Task
Analyse each listing above as a long-term rental (12-month leases) to be bought with 20% down and a 30-year mortgage. Apply exactly the same assumptions, scorecard and red flags to every property so that homes in different states compare on equal terms. For each property:
1.	Verify the listing facts and the current status (active, pending or under contract) from the listing site and the builder or county records.
2.	Determine a realistic monthly rent from market evidence (section 4).
3.	Run the investment analysis at the standard assumptions (sections 2 and 5).
4.	Score the 21 factors in section 6, giving the source and date behind each score.
5.	Check the red flags in section 7. Any red flag overrides the score.
6.	Find the listing agent, builder and HOA contacts and draft the three emails in section 9.
7.	Recommend a decision and the maximum price worth paying (section 10).
8.	Deliver the result in the format set out in section 11.
State every assumption. Label estimates as estimates. Where a fact cannot be verified, say so and put the question in the emails instead of guessing.

3. Property snapshot
Record for each home:
•	Address, listing link and listing status. If under contract, whether backup offers are accepted.
•	Builder or seller, community and plan/elevation.
•	New build, under construction or resale; year built or completion date.
•	Beds, baths and garage; living area and lot size.
•	List price, price per sq ft, days on market and price history.
•	Incentives (rate buydown, closing credit, upgrades), whether they apply to investors, and the effective price after incentives.

•	A daylight photograph of the full front elevation.
4. Realistic rent
Use at least three independent sources:
•	Automated estimate: Zillow Rent Zestimate or Rentometer. Treat it as a ceiling, not a target.
•	Comparable rentals (3 to 5): within 1 mile, preferably the same subdivision or school district; same beds and baths; living area within 15%; built within the last 10 years. Record address, rent, size, distance and days on market.
•	Property managers (1 or 2): their achievable rent and typical vacancy for the home.
Then:
•	Report a conservative and a realistic rent. Underwrite at the realistic rent: the median of the sources, rounded down to the nearest $25.
•	Comparables listed for more than 45 days mean the asking rents are too high; 60 days or more is a red flag.
•	Adjust for supply: many investor-owned or build-to-rent homes in the community compress rents.
•	Never rely on the seller's or builder's rent figure alone.
5. Investment analysis
Calculate, monthly and annually:
•	Cash invested = down payment + closing costs + make-ready − confirmed credits.
•	Principal and interest (P&I) = L × r(1 + r)^360 ÷ [(1 + r)^360 − 1], where L = 80% of price and r = 6.5% ÷ 12.
•	Effective rent = rent × (1 − vacancy).
•	Net operating income (NOI) = effective rent − (tax + insurance + HOA + maintenance + management + other costs such as lawn care, pest control and landlord-paid utilities). NOI excludes the mortgage.
•	Cash flow = NOI − P&I.
•	Cap rate = annual NOI ÷ price. Cash-on-cash return = annual cash flow ÷ cash invested.
•	Debt service coverage (DSCR) = NOI ÷ P&I. 1.25 or more is strong; 1.15 to 1.25 acceptable; 1.00 to 1.15 tight; below 1.00 the rent does not cover the debt.
•	Gross yield = annual rent ÷ price, with the monthly rent-to-price ratio.
•	Break-even rent (cash flow of $0) and break-even occupancy.
•	Maximum price for $0 cash flow at the realistic rent, and the discount this needs from the list price.
•	Price sensitivity: cash flow and cash-on-cash at the list price and at 20k, 40k below it.
•	Stress test: rent 10% lower; vacancy 10%; maintenance 1.5% of price; tax 10% higher; insurance 20% higher; all of these combined.
•	5-year view: cumulative cash flow, principal paid down and appreciation at 3% a year, less 7% selling costs, with the annualised return on cash invested.
At these assumptions a home usually needs monthly rent of about 1% of its price to break even. A home whose rent does not cover P&I, tax, insurance, HOA, vacancy and maintenance cannot score above 4 for cash flow, however good the house.
6. Scorecard (100 points)

Decision rule. 80 or more: strong buy candidate. 70 to 79: buy candidate; negotiate price, rate buydown or closing credit. 55 to 69: conditional; proceed only if negotiation closes the cash-flow gap. Below 55: pass.
7. Red flags
Any one of these means do not proceed until it is resolved, whatever the score:
•	The HOA bans, caps or restricts long-term leasing, including owner-occupancy periods.
•	FEMA zone A, AE, V or VE without affordable flood cover, or no affordable landlord wind or flood cover.
•	Cash flow worse than −$300 a month at the realistic rent, with no realistic path to break-even within 3 years.
•	Comparable rentals sitting 60 days or more unleased, or heavy investor or build-to-rent saturation.
•	A builder with unresolved structural-defect litigation or a pattern of withheld incentives.
•	An HOA in litigation or facing a large special assessment.
•	A structural, foundation or water-intrusion defect that has not been remedied.
8. Documents and inspections
Obtain these before the option period ends and the earnest money becomes non-refundable:
•	Financing: an investor (non-owner-occupied) loan quote in writing with rate, points, APR and lock period, comparing the builder's lender with an outside lender on total cost; the lender's acceptance of foreign-sourced funds and its source-of-funds document list; the Loan Estimate checked against the 3% closing-cost assumption.
•	Title, tax and HOA: the title commitment (easements, liens, MUD/PID notices); tax by entity on the completed, non-homestead value; the HOA declaration (CC&Rs), bylaws, budget, reserve study and resale certificate; leasing permission in writing; planned special assessments or fee increases.
•	Condition and warranty: for a new build, independent pre-drywall and final inspections, the 11-month warranty inspection and the builder's 1-2-10 warranty with its claim process. For a resale, a full inspection, sewer scope, termite (WDI) report, roof, HVAC and water-heater ages, the seller's disclosure and a CLUE claims report. For both, the survey or plot plan showing setbacks, easements, drainage and lot grade.
•	Risk and insurance: confirmation of the FEMA zone, an elevation certificate near a flood zone, and landlord (DP-3) quotes including wind and flood.
•	Rental readiness: the property manager's agreement with management, leasing and renewal fees; city or county rental registration rules; appliances and blinds included or budgeted; utility providers and MUD water and sewer rates.
9. Contacts and emails
For each property, find and show beside its emails:
•	Listing agent: name, brokerage, email and phone.
•	Builder sales office: consultant names, email, phone, office address and hours.
•	HOA management company: association name, manager, email and phone.
Give the source of every contact detail. When an email or phone number is not published, say so and say how to obtain it (the listing site's contact form, the sales office, or the HOA management certificate recorded with the county). Never invent an address. On builder inventory the listing agent usually lists for the builder, so send the builder email first and copy the agent.
Write three emails per property. They must be weighty, brief and precise, so that the recipient treats them as the conditions of a serious buyer:
•	The subject starts with the street address and states the purpose and the reply date.
•	One opening sentence: buying as a long-term rental, 20% down, financing arranged, decision pending their written answers.
•	6 to 9 numbered, decision-critical questions, one line each, most important first.
•	Answers and documents requested in writing by a set date, 3 business days ahead.
•	One closing line committing to the next step.
•	Signature: M. Mohsin Chowdhury, MMS Consortium, mms221@gmail.com, [phone].

10. Final summary and recommendation
Show a side-by-side table of all properties: list price, realistic rent, cash invested, monthly cash flow, cap rate, cash-on-cash, DSCR, break-even rent, maximum price for $0 cash flow, weighted score out of 100, red flags and recommendation.
For each property give:
•	The recommendation: strong buy; buy with negotiation; buy only at or below the maximum price; buy only with a financing incentive; keep as backup; or do not buy.
•	A price verdict against sold comparables and the maximum price for $0 cash flow: below market, fair, slightly overpriced or significantly overpriced.
•	The maximum price I should pay.
•	The main reasons to buy, the main risks, and the conditions to meet before proceeding.
11. Output format
Deliver two outputs with the same content:
1.	An interactive HTML artifact. Put the summary and recommendation first, then the side-by-side comparison with the front-elevation photos, an editable calculator (assumptions, price and rent) that recalculates every metric, the scorecard, red flags, document checklist, and the three emails per property with each party's email address and phone number and a copy button.
2.	A PDF in A4 portrait with the page number on every page ("Page X of Y"), laid out to print cleanly.
Conventions: US dollars; temperatures in °C; dates written as day, month and year; every figure sourced and dated; estimates labelled.
