1 · Summary and recommendation

The finding, in one paragraph

Not one of the twelve listings covers its own costs as a long-term rental at the standard assumptions. Cash flow ranges from −$1,429 to −$378 a month. Cap rates run 0.72% to 4.44% against debt costing 6.5%, so every property is negatively levered. The highest score in the set is 52.8 out of 100, which is below the template's own pass mark of 55, so the decision rule in section 6 returns Pass for all twelve.

11 of the 12 cannot reach break-even at any mortgage interest rate. For those properties, net operating income after tax, insurance, HOA, vacancy, maintenance and management is less than the principal repayment alone on an interest-free 30-year loan. A rate buydown cannot rescue them; only a materially lower purchase price or materially higher rent can.

Why the economics fail

Three costs do the damage, and two of them are specific to the communities chosen.

Property tax

Five of the twelve sit in Texas MUD districts where the builder's own published rate is 3.00% to 3.55% of value a year — above the 2.98% the template already treats as a score of 4 out of 10. On a $280,000 home a 3.37% rate is $786 a month before a single repair.

HOA assessments

The Lennar masterplans charge $183.33 to $191.66 a month — $2,200 to $2,300 a year, against the template's benchmark of $75 a month for a passing score. That is roughly 10% of gross rent going to the association.

Rent-to-price

The template notes a home usually needs monthly rent near 1% of price to break even. The best in this set reaches 0.860% and the worst 0.554%. These are $250,000–$360,000 houses in towns whose evidenced market rents are $1,400 to $2,400.

What is wrong with the list itself

  • The 14 screenshots contain 12 unique properties. IMG_3503 duplicates IMG_3490 (117 Bayou Bend Ct, Angleton), and IMG_3506 is a front-elevation render with no link attached.
  • Two of the twelve are not homes. Stable View (Lafayette, LA) and Crest at Morganfield (Lake Charles, LA) are builder community pages covering price ranges of $230,500–$323,500 and $233,500–$324,500. There is no address, lot, plan or price to underwrite. Both are shown below for completeness using the midpoint of the advertised range, clearly marked as an estimate.
  • Two more appear already gone. 173 Cotton Cv (Snook) and 6515 Little Yellow Ct (Needville) are absent from their builders' current published inventories as at 4 October 2026, although marketing flyers for both still exist. Confirm status before spending anything further.
  • So 8 of the 12 are actually live, priced, specific homes that can be analysed as the template intends.
  • Only 3 of the 12 images are daylight photographs of the completed front elevation. Seven are builder renderings and one was taken at dusk. Section 3 requires a daylight photograph of the full front elevation, so eight need replacing with site photographs.
  • The brief is headed "14homehouston", but four of the twelve are not in the Houston market — Farmersville and Cleburne are Dallas–Fort Worth, Snook and Caldwell are Bryan–College Station, and two are in Louisiana.

Where the data came from, and what is still missing

Zillow returns HTTP 403 to this environment, as do Trulia, HAR and NewHomeSource. Listing facts were therefore verified against the builders' own websites and Redfin, and flood zones were queried directly from FEMA. That makes the price, specification, HOA, tax and flood data stronger than a Zillow scrape would have been — but it leaves real gaps, and those gaps are named rather than filled with guesses.

  • HOA leasing rules. Not one declaration, resale certificate or written leasing permission has been seen. The template makes this a blocking red flag for all twelve.

7 · Method and limitations

How every number was produced

Inputs live in data/properties.json, each with its source and retrieval date. engine.py implements sections 5, 6, 7 and 10 of the template and generate.py renders this page and the PDF. Re-running python3 generate.py reproduces both deliverables exactly. The calculator on this page uses the same formulas in JavaScript, so the figures you see when you change an input are computed the same way as the printed ones.

Formulas, as specified in section 5

  • Principal and interest = L · r(1+r)360 ÷ [(1+r)360 − 1], L = 80% of price, r = 6.5% ÷ 12
  • Effective rent = rent × (1 − vacancy); management = 8% of rent collected, i.e. of effective rent
  • NOI = effective rent − (tax + insurance + HOA + maintenance + management + other); NOI excludes the mortgage
  • Cash flow = NOI − P&I · Cap rate = annual NOI ÷ price · Cash-on-cash = annual cash flow ÷ cash invested · DSCR = NOI ÷ P&I
  • Break-even rent = fixed costs ÷ [(1 − management) × (1 − vacancy)]
  • Maximum price at $0 cash flow solves for price with tax, insurance, maintenance and debt all scaling with price

Limitations you should hold against this document

  • Rents are market-level medians, not matched comparables, and no property manager has confirmed them.
  • Seven of 21 scored factors are provisional for every property; the per-property scorecard tags each one.
  • Insurance at 0.6% of price is a template default, not a quote, and is materially optimistic for the two Louisiana entries and for Angleton.
  • Tax rates for 7 of 12 properties are the template's 2.98% assumption, not a verified rate. Where a MUD or PID exists the true rate will be higher, which makes those seven look better than they are.
  • No site visit, inspection, title search or HOA document review has been carried out.
  • Flood zones for 6 of 12 rest on community or city coordinates rather than the exact lot. Four locations have Special Flood Hazard Area polygons nearby and need a determination for the specific lot.
